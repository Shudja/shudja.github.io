*"CEO and Academic Mismatch"*
(with Jason Aimone and Blaine McCormick)
**Industry and Higher Education**, 2024.

---

Business schools train students for careers in business. CEOs run businesses. You'd think the two would be well-aligned. But are the skills that CEOs say they want in new hires the same skills that academic programs emphasize?

This survey paper investigates the gap between what business executives report valuing in graduates and what higher education institutions prioritize in their curricula — and what that mismatch means for students and schools.

### The Mismatch

The paper draws on survey evidence from CEOs and senior executives about the competencies they most value: things like judgment under uncertainty, communication, adaptability, ethical reasoning, and collaboration. It then examines how these align with what business programs actually emphasize — a curriculum often dominated by technical skills, disciplinary knowledge, and measurable outcomes that are easier to teach and assess.

The gap the paper documents isn't just anecdotal. Executives consistently report that new hires lack certain soft and integrative skills, even when those hires have strong technical credentials. Meanwhile, academic programs may be optimizing for rankings metrics and research prestige in ways that don't fully align with preparing students for leadership.

### Why It Matters

The stakes of this mismatch are high on multiple sides. Students invest enormous resources in business education expecting it to prepare them for careers. Firms invest in hiring and training, partly to compensate for gaps left by formal education. And universities face pressure to demonstrate relevance and return on investment.

The paper argues that closing the gap requires both clearer signals from the business community about what matters and greater willingness from academic institutions to treat practical judgment and leadership as learnable, assessable skills rather than afterthoughts.
